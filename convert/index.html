Beach Cart Conversion

You already own a beach cart or wagon. You want it to drive itself across the sand instead of dragging your shoulder out of its socket. This page is the honest version of how that conversion works — from the builder of the AUTO-CART, the fully autonomous beach cart 1.5 million people watched follow its owner down the beach.

The part that kills most conversions

It isn't the motor. It's the wheels. Soft balloon tires grip sand far better than they grip a smooth plastic hub — so the first time real motor torque hits, the hub spins inside the tire. On most balloon tires the valve stem routes through the hub, which means one good slip shears the stem clean off and the tire is dead on the beach. It's the most common field failure on kit-built and home-converted carts, and almost nobody warns you about it.

Two things prevent it. First, a hub that locks the tire mechanically, not just by friction. Second, the right adhesive bond as insurance. (A side note worth knowing: on Wheeleez tires the valve stem exits outside the hub — so even if slip happens, nothing tears. That detail is a real part of what their premium price buys.)

The rest of the conversion

Hubs keep the wheels alive; the drivetrain makes them turn. Honest expectations

A conversion done right — locked hubs, adequate motors, LiFePO4 power — turns a dead-weight wagon into a machine that hauls 400 lb across soft sand while you walk. Done wrong, it eats tires and strands you at the far end of the beach. The difference is about $24 of hub files and reading the drivetrain chapter before buying a motor kit.
