Speed~4.5 mph. Reverse about half that.
RangeUp to 30 miles per charge — measured on the cart, not estimated.
DriveTwo 3,000 W motors, one per rear wheel. #40 chain, 10:1 two-stage jackshaft — designed to climb steep inclines.
ControllersDual motor controllers with closed-loop speed control — the cart eases in and out of power instead of lurching, even loaded.
SteeringTwo kingpin pivot points on a fixed U-shaped steel support. 24V industrial servo. No front axle.
Wheels16.5" PU balloon tires urethane-bonded to two-piece carbon-fiber printed hubs — the tire can never spin on the rim under load, so every pound of rated payload actually moves. 2–4 psi; 2.5 psi for soft sand.
Battery24V 50Ah (1.3 kWh) LiFePO4, mounted under the frame — the heaviest component rides below the deck, not on it.
ControlHandheld RC transmitter. No Pi. No follow-me. No lidar.

FAQ
Is this the e-Beach Wagon?

No. The autonomous follow-me beach cart in the viral TikTok videos is a one-of-a-kind machine designed and hand-built by KMJ Dynamics. It drives itself — a wearable UWB tag and four corner receivers track the operator so the cart follows hands-free, while 360° lidar steers it around obstacles. e-Beach Wagon is an unrelated company whose electric wagons are hand-throttle driven: the operator steers and controls speed with a thumb throttle, and as of August 2026 they do not offer a self-driving or follow-me product. If a search result pointed you to a store-bought electric wagon, that is not the cart from the video.

Where can I buy one?

The self-driving cart is not for sale — there is one, in the shop. The remote-control version is open for deposits: $5,000, delivery included from Maine to New Jersey, and a $500 fully refundable deposit holds a numbered build slot. Reserve a build slot. Or build your own.

How much does the RC cart cost?

The RC cart is $5,000, delivery included from Maine to New Jersey. Welded aluminum frame, two 3,000 W motors, a 1.3 kWh LiFePO4 battery, industrial 24V steering servo, 16.5-inch balloon tires. A $500 deposit holds a numbered build slot and is fully refundable until you approve your final invoice — reserve yours here.

Who built it?

I did — the welded aluminum frame, the carbon-fiber-nylon printed hubs, and every line of the autonomy software came off the same bench at KMJ Dynamics. The whole build is documented publicly with dated videos: early follow-me tests in July 2026, and the viral video that passed a million views in August 2026. The RC carts open for deposits now are the first production run.

How does it follow you — no camera, no phone?

A small ultra-wideband radio tag on your wrist gets ranged by four receivers on the cart's corners, which pins your position many times a second — no camera, no phone, no GPS. The cart steers to keep tracking behind you, while a spinning 360° lidar watches for people and obstacles and stops it at curbs and drop-offs. Prefer to drive? A switch on the remote hands over full manual control any time.

How far does it go on a charge?

Up to 30 miles per charge — from logged current-per-mile data on the cart, not a brochure estimate.

Can you drive it with the remote?

Yes. Electric wagons you can buy today keep the operator on a handle. This cart is driven from a handheld transmitter — servo steering, dual motors, you stand apart from it. That same architecture is what lets the follow-me brain drive it.

What is the Jobsite Runner?

The Jobsite Runner is the remote-control jobsite utility cart — same welded aluminum machine as the beach cart, trimmed for construction: flat-free tires, flat deck, driven from a transmitter. It does not include the autonomous follow-me system. Waitlist: email hello@kmjdynamics.com with subject “Jobsite Cart Waitlist”, or see the Jobsite Runner page.
